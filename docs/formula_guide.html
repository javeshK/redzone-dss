1. The one idea that unlocks everything

Judges hear “formula” and think physics. Ours is closer to a weighted report card.

  • Every input is forced into the range 0 to 1.
  • 1 = more of that thing (steeper, wetter, more people, safer site — depending on the factor).
  • We multiply each factor by a weight (importance) and add.
  • Weights for one formula always add to 1.00, so the result also stays in 0–1.

Example you already understand: exam marks.

Final = 0.45×math + 0.40×physics + 0.15×english

That is exactly how landslide hazard is built. Math → slope, physics → past landslides, english → rainfall.

Say to a judge: “This is multi-criteria analysis, not a trained neural network. Weights are expert defaults in weights.yaml, not fitted AUC.”

2. What happens, in order

  1. Map the ground. DEM → slope. Streams → distance. GSI points → landslide density. Rainfall raster → how wet the climate signal is.
  2. Turn each map into 0–1. Steep becomes S. Landslide density becomes L. Rain becomes R. Streams/wetness become W.
  3. Per pixel, compute H_ls, then H_ff, then combine into H. Colour the pixel.
  4. Per village, average H inside the village (H_hab), compute social vulnerability V, then priority P.
  5. Per candidate site, throw away unsafe/steep/tiny sites, estimate how many people fit (C), then rank remaining sites with U_ij.

Expensive GIS is done offline in scripts 01–05. The dashboard mostly displays answers that were already computed.

3. Clip and min-max (the two tools)

Clip — “don’t let it go outside 0–1”

clip(x, 0, 1) means: if x is negative, use 0. If x is bigger than 1, use 1. Otherwise keep x.

x before clipafter clip
-0.20
0.40.4
1.71

Min-max — “rescale this district so the smallest is 0 and the largest is 1”

score = (value − min) / (max − min)

If rainfall in the district runs from 800 mm to 1800 mm, then 1300 mm becomes (1300−800)/(1800−800) = 0.50.

Judge trap: a 0.8 in Rudraprayag is not a probability, and it is not comparable to Chamoli until you freeze the same min and max. These scores are relative inside one district.
Q. Slope is 10°. Formula (slope−15)/30 = −0.167. What do we store?
0. Clip to 0–1. Gentle slopes are “no extra landslide severity from slope.”
Q. Why not use raw metres and millimetres in the weighted sum?
Because 40° and 1200 mm are different units. Adding them is meaningless. 0–1 makes weights mean “importance,” not “unit conversion.”

4. Ingredients of hazard: S, L, R, W

S — slope severity

S = clip( (slope_degrees − 15) / 30 , 0, 1 )
  • 15° or flatter → S = 0 (we treat it as not contributing).
  • 45° or steeper → S = 1 (as bad as this term gets).
  • 30° → (30−15)/30 = 0.50.

Why 15 and 45? Himalayan planning rule of thumb: below ~15° is more buildable; above ~45° is severely hostile. They are policy knobs, not fitted science.

SlopeSMeaning
10°0.00No slope penalty
15°0.00Start of penalty
25°0.33Moderate
30°0.50Halfway
45°1.00Maxed out
60°1.00Still 1 — clip

L — landslide density (from GSI points)

  1. Put each GSI landslide point on the raster grid (like dropping a 1 in that cell).
  2. Blur with a Gaussian whose width is about 500 m (KDE). Nearby cells light up too — a landslide is not only the exact pin.
  3. Min-max the blurred map to 0–1 across the district. That is L.

If there are no GSI points, fallback: treat slopes steeper than 30° as a proxy. Label it DERIVED, never “official GSI zonation.”

R — rainfall severity

Take a rainfall raster (CHIRPS / ERA5, or orographic fallback from DEM). Min-max to 0–1 (or scale millimetres by a cap). High R = wetter / more extreme in our chosen statistic, relative to this district.

The UI slider does not download a new storm. It multiplies this R by 1.2 or 1.5, clips to 1, and recomputes H. That is a scenario, not an IMD forecast.

W — wetness / flood-path

Two ideas glued together:

  1. Near a stream is worse. Distance capped at 1 km:
    stream_score = clip(1 − dist_m / 1000, 0, 1)
    On the bank (0 m) → 1. At 1 km or further → 0.
  2. TWI (topographic wetness) — cells that collect water and are not steep. Rough idea: TWI ≈ ln(catchment / tan(slope)). Then min-max TWI to 0–1.

W is “how much this pixel looks like a cloudburst path,” not a HEC-RAS flood depth.

Q. A house is 250 m from a nala. What is stream_score?
1 − 250/1000 = 0.75.
Q. Is TWI a flood model?
No. No hydrograph, no Manning n, no inundation depth. Susceptibility proxy only.

5. Landslide score H_ls

H_ls = 0.45 × S + 0.40 × L + 0.15 × R

Read it as: slope is the biggest driver (45%), then “this neighbourhood already had slides” (40%), then rain as a trigger (15%).

Worked example. S = 0.80, L = 0.60, R = 0.50

H_ls = 0.45×0.80 + 0.40×0.60 + 0.15×0.50 = 0.360 + 0.240 + 0.075 = 0.675

0.675 means “fairly high landslide susceptibility on our index,” still not a 67.5% chance of a slide tomorrow.

Say: “H_ls is a 0–1 susceptibility index, not a probability of failure.”

Calculator — H_ls

Q. Why is rainfall only 0.15 in H_ls if rain triggers slides?
Because the same R also enters flash-flood. Slope + inventory already carry most of the landslide story. Rain is the shared trigger, not the whole landslide map.

6. Flash-flood score H_ff

H_ff = 0.50 × W + 0.50 × R

Half “are you in the water’s path?”, half “is the rainfall signal high?”

Worked example. W = 0.70, R = 0.50

H_ff = 0.50×0.70 + 0.50×0.50 = 0.35 + 0.25 = 0.60
This is not river flooding on the Ganga plains. It is Himalayan cloudburst / stream-corridor susceptibility.

Calculator — H_ff

Q. Where is discharge / return period in H_ff?
Nowhere. No 1-in-100 year. If you say “100-year flood,” they will eat you.

7. Combined hazard H (the formula everyone asks about)

H = 1 − (1 − H_ls) × (1 − H_ff)

This is the “at least one of the two is dangerous” rule, also called a probabilistic OR.

Intuition without probability jargon

Think of (1 − H_ls) as “how landslide-safe is this pixel.” (1 − H_ff) is “how flood-safe.” Multiply safeties: you are only fully safe if both are safe. Then H is 1 minus that combined safety.

Worked example (same numbers as the unit test): H_ls = 0.78, H_ff = 0.61

H = 1 − (1−0.78)×(1−0.61) = 1 − (0.22)×(0.39) = 1 − 0.0858 = 0.9142

Notice H is higher than both inputs. Combining two medium threats makes a high combined threat. That is the point: either can kill you.

H_lsH_ffH (OR)max()average
0.600.600.840.600.60
0.900.100.910.900.50

Look at row 2: average would hide a 0.90 landslide behind a “0.50 overall.” OR and max do not hide it. We picked OR because we treat the two hazards as able to strike together, not as “pick the worse one only.”

Honest limit: the formula assumes the two hazards are independent. They are not — both use rainfall. So H can be a bit inflated. We accept extra red (false alarm) over hidden green. If they ask for a cleaner model, say copulas / shared-factor — Phase 3, not this demo.

Calculator — H from H_ls and H_ff

Q. If H_ls = 0.6 and H_ff = 0.6, is the pixel Red?
H = 1 − 0.4×0.4 = 0.84, which is ≥ 0.70, so Red. Average would have been Orange. Know this example cold.
Q. Why not just add H_ls + H_ff?
That can exceed 1. Weights already used inside each hazard. OR keeps H in [0,1] and encodes “either hazard is enough.”

8. Painting the map: Red / Orange / Yellow / Green

After H is computed on every pixel:

If H is…Class
≥ 0.70Red
0.50 to < 0.70Orange
0.30 to < 0.50Yellow
< 0.30Green

We then turn red (and other class) cells into polygons, drop scraps smaller than 2 ha, simplify for the web map.

Habitation hazard H_hab = mean of H inside the village polygon (or sampled at the point). % red = share of that village’s area whose pixels are Red.

Never say: “These are NDMA / GSI official hazard zones.” Do say: “Derived screening index. Provenance DERIVED.”
KPI trap: meta.json field red_zone_area_ha currently sums several zone classes and can look larger than the district because polygons overlap. Do not quote “104% of district is red.” Talk about the colour on the map and the 0.70 rule.
Q. Who chose 0.70?
We did. It is a policy knob in weights.yaml, not a law. Changing it grows or shrinks red polygons.

9. Vulnerability V — “how badly will people be hurt if something happens”

Hazard is the mountain. Vulnerability is the household.

V = 0.25×population + 0.15×dependents + 0.20×isolation + 0.15×health_access + 0.25×historical_exposure

Each x is already 0–1 (high = more vulnerable, except health is coded so that far-from-hospital is worse in the engine).

FactorWeightWhat we actually used in the demo
Population size0.25Min-max of the 25 demo village populations. Bigger village → higher this factor.
Dependents0.15Proxy, not Census age tables. Smaller villages get a higher dependent-like score.
Isolation0.20Straight-line km to nearest of 4 hubs (Rudraprayag Town, Guptkashi, Agastyamuni, Srinagar) / 18 km, capped at 1.
Health access0.15Also from those hubs (not a real PHC inventory distance).
Historical exposure0.25GSI points within 1 km, plus a +0.35 bump if the village is on our 2013 Kedarnath-corridor list.

Missing data: renormalize

If dependents is missing, we do not pretend it is 0. We drop it and stretch the remaining weights so they still add to 1.

Example: only population (0.25) and isolation (0.20) present. Total = 0.45.

new weight_pop = 0.25/0.45 = 0.556 new weight_iso = 0.20/0.45 = 0.444 V = 0.556×pop + 0.444×isolation
Say: “Missing factor → drop and renormalize. We do not silently treat missing as safe.”

Calculator — V (all five factors present)

Q. Is isolation road-network distance?
No. Haversine (straight line). Winter road cuts are not modelled.
Q. Did we use Census 2011 age groups for dependents?
Not in the demo engine. Do not claim it. Call it a proxy.

10. Relocation priority P

Now mix “dangerous place” with “fragile people.”

P = 0.60 × H_hab + 0.40 × V

Hazard gets more weight than social vulnerability because a safe rich village still should not sit on a debris fan — and a fragile village on a safe bench is a different problem.

Worked example. H_hab = 0.82, V = 0.71

P = 0.60×0.82 + 0.40×0.71 = 0.492 + 0.284 = 0.776
PClassPlain meaning
≥ 0.75ImmediatePlan relocation / evacuation now
0.60 – < 0.75Short-termThis season
0.40 – < 0.60Medium-termMitigate and monitor
< 0.40MonitorStay, strengthen

0.776 → Immediate from the table. The explain panel shows:

  • multi_hazard contribution = 0.60 × 0.82 = 0.492
  • vulnerability contribution = 0.40 × 0.71 = 0.284

Those two contributions are P. That is what “explainable” means.

The override (this is a favourite judge question)

Even if P is only 0.50 (Medium-term), we force Immediate if either:

  • % of village area in Red ≥ 40%, or
  • H_hab ≥ 0.80

Why? A small village with low “social V” can still be sitting on a red fan. Physics first.

Demo script line: “Immediate is forced because 46% of habitation area falls in red zone.”

Calculator — P and class (includes override)

Q. H_hab = 0.70, V = 0.40, pct_red = 46. What class, and why?
P = 0.60×0.70 + 0.40×0.40 = 0.42 + 0.16 = 0.58 → would be Medium-term, but 46% ≥ 40% forces Immediate.
Q. Does the override change the number P?
No. It changes the label. P can still be 0.58. The dashboard label becomes Immediate. Explain panel adds a note with weight 0.

11. Site screening — throw away bad land first

Before ranking, a candidate must pass all of:

RuleThreshold
Mean hazard of site< 0.40
Mean slope< 20°
Protected share< 0.5 (treated as not a park)
Area≥ 3 hectares

This is a gate, not a score. Fail any one → site is not recommended.

Demo sites are EXPERT_SCREENED polygons, then checked against rasters. Only a handful survive in this terrain — that shortage is a finding.

Q. Why 20° to enter the list but 15° inside capacity?
Himalaya is steep; 20° lets a site exist. Capacity then penalises how much of it is actually <15° (more buildable).

12. Carrying capacity — how many people could physically fit

Not a legal number. Not forest clearance. First-order physical screening.

Step A — how much land is even usable?

A_safe (ha) = area_ha × p_buildable × p_slope_lt15 × (1 − p_hazard) × (1 − p_protected)

Each p is a fraction 0–1. Multiplying means: only the land that is buildable AND flatter AND not hazardous AND not protected counts.

Worked example. 16 ha site, buildable 0.68, slope<15 share 0.80, hazard 0.28, protected 0

A_safe = 16 × 0.68 × 0.80 × (1−0.28) × (1−0) = 16 × 0.68 × 0.80 × 0.72 = 6.266 ha

Step B — convert to people at 80 m² each

1 hectare = 10,000 m², so 6.266 ha = 62,660 m².

C_raw = A_safe_m² / 80 = 62660 / 80 = 783.25 people
Naming bug in YAML: the key is called ha_per_person: 80 but the code divides square metres by 80. So it is 80 m² per person, not 80 hectares per person. If you say hectares you fail the viva.

Step C — access deraters (roads, water, health each between 0.6 and 1.0 typically)

C = integer( C_raw × f_road × f_water × f_health )

Example: 0.90 × 0.85 × 0.80 = 0.612

C = int(783.25 × 0.612) = int(479.3) = 479

Step D — subtract people already there

C_available = max(0, C − existing_population)

If 180 people already live there: 479 − 180 = 299 spare.

Always say: “First-order physical screening capacity, not statutory settlement capacity.”

Calculator — capacity

Q. Why 80 m², not SPHERE ~45 m²?
A screening constant for a slightly generous plot+services campus. SDMA can change it in weights.yaml. It is not an Indian building bye-law.
Q. Can C_available go negative?
No. max(0, C − existing). We never recommend “minus 50 people of space.”

13. Site suitability U_ij — “why this site for this village”

i = habitation, j = site. Higher U is better.

U_ij = 0.30×safety + 0.15×distance + 0.15×road + 0.10×healthcare + 0.05×school + 0.10×water + 0.15×capacity_fit
TermHow it is 0–1
safety1 − site hazard. Hazard 0.18 → safety 0.82.
distance1 − (km between village and site) / 25. 5 km → 0.80. 25 km or more → 0.
road / health / school / waterAccess factors already 0–1 on the site (demo uses f_road etc.).
capacity_fitclip(C_available / village_population, 0, 1). If the site can hold the whole village, this is 1.

Hard constraint (not a weight): we only pick a site as the winner if

C_available ≥ 0.5 × village_population

So a tiny beautiful site that can take 10% of the village is not allowed to win alone. If nobody qualifies, we split across the top 2.

This is ranking, not an operations-research optimizer. There is no MIP, no OR-Tools. If the PPT says “Optimization,” say “weighted ranking with a capacity constraint.”

Worked example. safety 0.82, distance 0.80, road 0.90, health 0.80, school 0.70, water 0.85, cap_fit 1.00

U = 0.30×0.82 + 0.15×0.80 + 0.15×0.90 + 0.10×0.80 + 0.05×0.70 + 0.10×0.85 + 0.15×1.00 = 0.246 + 0.120 + 0.135 + 0.080 + 0.035 + 0.085 + 0.150 = 0.851

Calculator — U_ij

Q. Distance is 30 km. What is the distance score?
1 − 30/25 = −0.2 → clip to 0. We do not give negative suitability from distance; we just give no credit.
Q. Why is school only 0.05?
Nice to have for a screening relocate. Safety and “will they fit” matter more in the first pass.

14. One village, start to finish (memorise this story)

Invented numbers in the shape of the spec’s JSON example — good for a whiteboard.

  1. Pixel ingredients near the village: S=0.80, L=0.70, R=0.50, W=0.61.
  2. H_ls = 0.45×0.80 + 0.40×0.70 + 0.15×0.50 = 0.36 + 0.28 + 0.075 = 0.715.
  3. H_ff = 0.50×0.61 + 0.50×0.50 = 0.305 + 0.25 = 0.555.
  4. H = 1 − (1−0.715)×(1−0.555) = 1 − 0.285×0.445 = 1 − 0.1268 = 0.873 → Red pixel.
  5. Village mean H_hab ≈ 0.82, and 46% of its area is Red.
  6. V ≈ 0.71 from the five social factors.
  7. P = 0.60×0.82 + 0.40×0.71 = 0.776 ≥ 0.75 → Immediate. Even if P were lower, 46% red would force Immediate.
  8. Sites with hazard ≥ 0.40 or slope ≥ 20° are discarded.
  9. A remaining site gets C and C_available as in section 12.
  10. U_ij is computed for every surviving site. Winner must have spare capacity ≥ 50% of village pop. Show runner-up.
On demo day, click a real village and read its H, V, P, % red from the screen. If the screen disagrees with this story, the screen wins.
